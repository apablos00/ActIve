# Problem and evidence

Back to [[00 Home]]

**Students let AI read and think for them because their material is long and dull, so they finish faster and learn less.**

## Three anchors
| Source | What it says |
|---|---|
| **OECD Digital Education Outlook 2026** | General-purpose AI boosts performance, not learning. The edge disappears or flips in exams once AI is removed. In one study it features, students who practised with a plain chatbot scored up to 17% lower on the closed-book exam. The OECD asks for tools that question and nudge. |
| **HEPI Student GenAI Survey 2026** | 95% of UK students use AI. 94% use it for assessed work. Pasting AI text into assessed work went from 3% in 2024 to 12% in 2026. |
| **WEF Future of Jobs 2025** | 39% of core skills change by 2030. Analytical thinking is the top core skill for about 7 in 10 employers. |

## Context
Autumn 2026: hundreds of thousands of students rallied in France, Italy, Spain and Germany. The protests are about costs, funding and the future, not AI.
Say: *"Students are demanding a better education. We can't fix budgets. We can fix how they learn."*

## Q&A backup
- MIT Media Lab 2025: ChatGPT writers showed the weakest brain connectivity and couldn't quote their own essays. Small sample.
- Gerlich 2025: 666 people. Heavy AI use linked to lower critical thinking. Worst at ages 17-25. Correlation.
- Microsoft and CMU 2025: more trust in AI, less critical thinking.
